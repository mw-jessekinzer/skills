# Grill Me

## Overview

Interview the user relentlessly about their plan, idea, or decision until you both reach **90%+ shared understanding** — then, and only then, do the work. This is structured rubber-ducking: the questions surface the details, assumptions, and trade-offs the user hasn't said out loud yet.

The goal is alignment, not interrogation. You leave the session knowing exactly what to build, write, or recommend.

## The Rules

1. **One question at a time.** Ask a single question, wait for the answer, then ask the next. Never dump a list of questions. The user's answer shapes what you ask next.

2. **Walk the decision tree branch by branch.** Resolve dependencies in order — settle the upstream decision before the ones that hang off it. Don't jump around.

3. **For every question, give your recommended answer.** Make the call you'd make and say why in a sentence. This accelerates the conversation and gives the user something to push against. They can accept, reject, or refine.

4. **Look it up before you ask it.** If a question can be answered from the vault, existing notes, prior docs, a brief, or files already in context, go find the answer instead of asking. Only ask the user what only the user knows.

5. **Be relentless, not exhaustive.** Keep probing the things that actually change the outcome — scope, audience, success criteria, constraints, non-negotiables, what "done" looks like. Don't grind on trivia.

6. **Track the percentage.** Periodically note where you are ("~70% — still fuzzy on the audience and the deadline"). Stop asking when you hit ~90%+, or when the user calls it.

## Process

```
1. Restate the task in one line so you're grilling the right thing.
2. Ask one question (with your recommendation). Wait.
3. Repeat — each question informed by the last answer, dependencies first.
4. When confident gaps remain, fill them from notes/files instead of asking.
5. At ~90%+ shared understanding, STOP and write the Understanding Summary.
6. Confirm the summary with the user, then do the work.
```

## Understanding Summary

When you hit 90%+, stop interviewing and lay out what you now both know:

- **What we're doing** — the task in 1-2 sentences
- **Key decisions** — each resolved branch and the answer landed on
- **Constraints / non-negotiables** — what must be true
- **Success looks like** — how we'll know it worked
- **Open 10%** — what's still uncertain and why it's safe to proceed anyway

Then ask: "Does this match your understanding? Anything to fix before I start?"

## When NOT to Use

- The task is trivial or fully specified already — just do it.
- The user wants you to generate options, not be interviewed — that's a thinking-partner job, not grilling.
- Mid-execution requests for help — grilling is a front-end activity, before work starts.

## Common Mistakes

- **Asking five questions in one message.** Breaks the one-at-a-time rule and overwhelms. Ask one.
- **Asking what you could look up.** Check the vault/notes/files first.
- **No recommendation.** "What do you want for the headline?" is lazy. Offer your pick and reasoning.
- **Stopping too early.** A few questions in does not equal 90%. Keep going until the open questions are genuinely the safe-to-defer 10%.
- **Grinding past 90%.** Once aligned, stop and ship the summary. Don't interrogate for its own sake.
