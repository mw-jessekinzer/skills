# Midwestern — Q3 2026 Growth Marketing Strategy
### Quarter: August 1 – October 31, 2026
**Prepared for:** Director of Marketing, Midwestern
**Goal:** Double marketing-sourced pipeline AND double won pipeline vs. Q2 — with extremely qualified leads, not lead volume.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Midwestern's pipeline today is a relationship business with a marketing layer that barely participates: **~5% of 2026 YTD pipeline is marketing-sourced**, marketing-sourced deals average **$22K vs. $127K** for relationship deals, and **zero marketing-sourced deals have closed-won this year**. Meanwhile referrals, relationships, and customer upgrades produce 100% of wins.

That's not a criticism — it's the map. The strategy for Q3 is:

1. **Don't fight the relationship engine — instrument and amplify it.** The referral program, customer expansion, and exec networks are the fastest path to doubling won pipeline in-quarter. Research is unambiguous: for professional services, referrals/partners are the #1 revenue source (10% of pipeline volume → 31% of revenue, per Hinge/partner-program data), and demand creation started in August physically cannot close by October at 90–170 day cycles.
2. **Build the marketing-sourced engine on hand-raisers, not MQLs.** Deploy two qualification-by-design lead magnets (a Build-vs-Buy-vs-Staff-Aug cost calculator and a Vendor Evaluation Scorecard), convert the podcast into a guest-as-prospect ABM weapon, and run signal-based outbound + Thought Leader Ads against the vertical account lists. Target: **2x marketing-sourced pipeline created (≥$725K in Q3)** at a dramatically higher average deal size.
3. **Fix the middle of the funnel to hit the won-pipeline goal.** There is **$4.85M of open pipeline right now**; doubling Q3 wins (≥$1.08M) will come from converting what already exists — proposal/negotiation-stage enablement, case-study proof, reference programs, and a customer-expansion campaign — not from new top-of-funnel.

**Q3 targets (vs. Q2 actuals from HubSpot):**

| Metric | Q2 2026 actual | Q3 2026 target |
|---|---|---|
| Marketing-sourced pipeline created (LinkedIn + Inbound + new marketing programs) | $363K | **≥$725K** |
| Referral-program pipeline created | $587K | **≥$900K** |
| Closed-won (whole team) | $539K (7 deals) | **≥$1.08M** |
| Avg. marketing-sourced deal size | ~$33K | **≥$75K** |
| Qualified conversations (hand-raisers from ICP accounts) | not tracked | **25+ tracked** |

## 3. Research Findings I: Best B2B Demand Gen Strategies (Tech / High-ACV Services), 2025–2026

*Compiled by research agents from Gartner, 6sense, Hinge Research Institute, HockeyStack benchmarks, Refine Labs, and practitioner datasets. Full source URLs in Appendix. Benchmarks are mostly SaaS-derived; direction transfers to services, magnitudes may not.*

**The buying-behavior backdrop:** 94% of B2B buying groups pre-rank a preferred vendor **before** first contact and buy that favorite 77% of the time (6sense 2025). 67% of buyers now prefer a rep-free buying experience (Gartner 2026). Buyers spend only ~17% of the journey with vendors. Translation for a services firm: **you win or lose before the first call — the "expert visibility" layer is not optional.**

Ranked by evidence strength and fit for Midwestern:

| # | Strategy | Verdict for Midwestern | Time-to-pipeline |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Systematized referral & partner program** | #1 services revenue source (10% of pipeline → 31% of revenue). Fastest in-quarter lever. Formalize give-first cadences, partner fee structures (10–20% of first engagement is standard), quarterly partner touches. | **Fast (in-quarter)** |
| 2 | **Signal-based outbound** (replaces volume cold email) | 4–10% reply rates vs 0.5–2% cold. Dev-agency signals are unusually clean: engineering job postings (build-vs-buy moment), funding rounds, new CTO/VP Eng, eng layoffs (staff-aug demand). Sub-2-hour response to signals = +82% engagement. | **Fast (weeks)** |
| 3 | **Executive dinners / roundtables** | Highest creation→qualified conversion of any channel class (live events 5.5%, virtual 6.4% vs 4.8% avg — HockeyStack). 8–15 senior buyers, $5–20K/event, opportunities in 60–90 days. Best format for >60-day sales cycles. | **Fast (60–90 days)** |
| 4 | **Founder/exec-led LinkedIn organic** | The strongest services channel long-term (Hinge: high-growth firms grow 4x faster and lead with expertise visibility). Compounds over 2–4 quarters; amplifies every other play immediately. | Slow to source, fast to influence |
| 5 | **LinkedIn Thought Leader Ads** (boosting exec posts to named accounts) | 4.65% CTR vs 0.68% standard formats; ~$0.51–0.84 CPC vs $2.42. Put 25–50% of LinkedIn budget here. Viable from $3–10K/mo. | Medium (1–2 quarters) |
| 6 | **ABM-lite for the verticals** | 50–150 named accounts per vertical; account research + TL Ads air cover + signal-triggered outbound + podcast invitations. Skip the 6sense/Demandbase platform tier — process-heavy, not tool-heavy. | Medium |
| 7 | **Podcast as guest-as-prospect engine** | Podcasts show ~0% in software attribution but influence up to 53% of deals in self-reported data. The play: guest list = target account list. Downloads are irrelevant; 15+ repurposable assets per episode. | Medium (relationships in weeks) |
| 8 | **Demand-capture hygiene** (Clutch, branded search, site conversion, response SLA) | Necessary but not sufficient. Highest intent, smallest volume. Clutch profile + reviews refresh is table stakes for dev-shop buyers. | **Fast** |
| 9 | Branded community | **Skip.** Weakest evidence (only 24% of programs can quantify impact). Participate in existing communities instead. | Slow |

**Demand creation vs. capture split:** Refine Labs' benchmark is 60–70% of paid budget to demand creation / 30–40% capture — but that assumes a mature capture layer. Midwestern's capture layer is nearly empty (no lead magnets, no visible gated offers, thin directory presence), and services firms should treat **referral-network cultivation as a third bucket** (behaves like creation, converts like capture). Recommended Q3 effort split: **~40% capture + conversion repair, ~40% creation, ~20% referral/partner formalization** — shifting toward creation in Q4 once the capture layer exists.

**The MQL trap (why "extremely qualified" is the right instinct):** Average MQL→SQL conversion is ~13%; only 44% of MQLs are even accepted by sales; ~50% never get structured follow-up. Teams that chase MQL volume degrade quality and destroy sales trust. The modern standard (Refine Labs' "HIRO" model — High-Intent Revenue Opportunities — and hand-raiser qualification) counts only **declared intent** (booked a call, requested pricing, asked to talk) from **ICP-fit accounts** as sales-ready. Everything else is nurture audience, not a lead. For a services firm this is natural: deals start as conversations, not trials. **Recommendation: Midwestern should never adopt MQL scoring. Track qualified conversations and pipeline $ by self-reported source instead.**

## 4. Research Findings II: Lead Magnets That Actually Produce Qualified B2B Leads

*Key evidence: interactive tools convert 30–50%+ vs 3–8% for gated PDFs; SQL conversion is ~48% higher for interactive tools than static assets; industry benchmark reports convert 14–28% for agencies; generic ebooks and value-less webinars are the worst performers (4–12%). Full examples and URLs in Appendix.*

### The qualification-by-design principle
The way to get *extremely qualified* leads from lead magnets is to build magnets **only a real buyer would want**:
- **Pricing/cost curiosity is itself high intent.** Cost calculators and pricing guides attract people budgeting a real project.
- **Require inputs only a buyer has.** A calculator asking team size, stack, and timeline filters out browsers automatically.
- **Vendor-evaluation formats are inherently self-qualifying** — only someone actively running a selection process downloads an RFP scorecard.
- **Form friction is a qualification lever.** Each well-chosen field (company size, dev headcount) trades volume for fit — which is exactly the trade Midwestern wants.
- **Behavioral data beats form data.** Calculator inputs tell sales what the prospect is actually planning; adding behavioral/intent signals to qualification lifts MQL→SQL ~70% in benchmark data.

### Proven formats and real examples

| Format | Real-world proof | Conversion benchmark |
|---|---|---|
| **Grader/assessment tools** | HubSpot Website Grader: 10M+ leads lifetime; gate converts 30–40% because the user invests effort first. WordStream's Ads Grader doubled as an annual benchmark-research engine. | 30–50%; quizzes in B2B professional services avg ~38% |
| **Build-vs-Buy / TCO calculators** | Already proven *specifically by dev agencies*: Brilworks, ZTABS (5-yr TCO + break-even), Neontri, Hatchworks. Belkins' cost calculator = their highest-converting page, 30% of all site conversions. | ROI calculators >11–30% |
| **Vendor evaluation scorecards / RFP templates** | Info-Tech Research Group's entire GTM runs on gated "blueprints" funneling into $100K+ advisory. Nvelop, Cognism, VendorSage publish weighted vendor scorecards as magnets. | High intent; low volume by design |
| **Original benchmark research** | ThoughtWorks Technology Radar (brand play, ungated); DORA State of DevOps (annual capture moment); Databox Benchmark Groups. | 14–28% for agency-published research |
| **Free audit/review offers** | Insites audit-widget clients: +500 leads/mo for one, $20K+ deals closed off audits alone. Devox's technical-debt audit tool (architectural risk graph) is a productized grader for legacy codebases. | Strongest BOFU converter for agencies |
| Generic ebooks / passive webinars | — | 4–12% (worst performers — avoid) |

**Gating in 2025–2026:** hybrid is the consensus — keep ~65–75% of content ungated (thought leadership, technical explainers) to build reach and trust; gate only high-specificity MOFU/BOFU assets (calculators, scorecards, benchmark data). C-level tolerance for gates is low (~28%); technical evaluators accept gates for deep implementation content (~68%). Gate the *deep output*, not the *entry experience*.

### The three lead magnets Midwestern should build

**Build #1 (August): "Build vs. Buy vs. Embed" Cost Calculator** — interactive, on-site.
Inputs: project type, team size needed, timeline, stack, in-house capacity. Output: 3-scenario cost comparison (hire FTEs / big-firm project / Midwestern embedded team) with 12-month TCO and break-even. Gate the detailed PDF breakdown + "review my numbers with an architect" CTA; show headline numbers ungated. **This is the single most on-the-nose proven format for this exact business** — and building a genuinely excellent interactive tool is itself a demo of Midwestern's craft. Every input is a qualification field, and sales receives the prospect's actual project parameters with the hand-raise.

**Build #2 (September): "Custom Software Partner Evaluation Scorecard"** — weighted-criteria spreadsheet/notion doc + short guide.
Criteria: technical depth, delivery track record, engagement flexibility, US-led accountability, security posture, AI-era practices. Only buyers actively comparing vendors want this — the definitive self-qualifying asset — and the criteria (honestly framed) mirror Midwestern's strengths. Cheap to produce (1–2 weeks), pairs with case studies in every sales conversation and outbound sequence.

**Build #3 (October, feeds Q4): Vertical delivery-maturity assessment** — 10–12 question scored assessment ("Software Delivery Readiness Score") deployed per-vertical for the ABM motion, with tiered results driving segmented nurture. Diagnostic quizzes perform best precisely in complex, long-cycle B2B sales. If Q3 capacity is tight, this slips to Q4 without harming the quarter.

*Deliberately not building: generic ebooks, "ultimate guides," passive webinar decks — the evidence says they produce the junk-lead volume this strategy exists to avoid.*

## 5. The Q3 Play Stack

Seven plays, ordered by expected in-quarter impact. Effort assumes current team + fractional design/dev support from the bench.

### Play 1 — Systematize the referral program *(Won-pipeline + pipeline lever · owner: Marketing + leadership)*
The referral program exists; Q3 makes it an engine.
- Formalize the partner tier: fee structure (10–20% of first engagement), simple one-page terms, target list of 20 natural partners (design studios, PE/VC operating partners, fractional CTOs, complementary agencies — data/security/ERP, platform ecosystems).
- Give-first cadence: Hinge's research shows top referral *givers* receive 24+ referrals per 6 months. Build a monthly rhythm of sending business/intros outward, tracked in HubSpot.
- Client-referral moments: instrument the delivery process — ask at go-live and at QBRs, not randomly. Every closed-won project generates a referral ask within 30 days.
- **Target: referral pipeline $587K → $900K+ in Q3.**

### Play 2 — Ship the calculator + scorecard, wired to hand-raiser flow *(Marketing-sourced pipeline lever · owner: Marketing)*
Per Section 4. Distribution matters more than the asset: each magnet launches with a 4-week promotion arc — founder LinkedIn posts, Thought Leader Ads to vertical account lists, outbound sequences referencing it, podcast mentions. Route every gated conversion to a human response **within 1 hour** (53% vs 17% conversion at 1hr vs 24hr).
- **Target: 8–12 qualified conversations, 4–6 opportunities, $300–450K pipeline by Oct 31.**

### Play 3 — Convert the podcast into a guest-as-prospect ABM engine *(Pipeline + relationship lever · owner: Marketing)*
Stop measuring downloads. Start measuring guest pipeline.
- Book 6–8 episodes in Q3 where guests are CTOs/VPs Eng **from the vertical target-account lists** (or referral-partner personas).
- Each episode → 15+ repurposed assets: clips for founder LinkedIn, TL Ad creative, vertical nurture emails.
- Pre/post-episode motion: research brief before, personal follow-up + intro asks after. The episode is the excuse for the relationship.
- **Target: 6+ target-account exec relationships, 2–3 opportunities sourced or accelerated.**

### Play 4 — ABM-lite + signal-based outbound on the verticals *(Marketing-sourced pipeline lever · owner: Marketing + SDR/sales)*
The vertical pages exist but are not yet discoverable in search indexes — so in Q3 the vertical motion must be **direct**, not organic.
- 50–100 named accounts per priority vertical (pick 2 verticals max for Q3 — focus beats coverage).
- Stand up signal monitoring (Clay-class tooling, $2–5K/mo): engineering job postings, funding events, new CTO/VP Eng hires, eng-team layoffs, stack-migration signals. Signal → personalized outreach within 2 hours where possible.
- TL Ads air cover to those account lists (25–50% of LinkedIn budget; start ~$4–6K/mo total paid).
- Also: basic SEO/indexing hygiene on the vertical pages so organic discovery exists by Q4.
- **Target: 12–15 qualified meetings, $250–350K pipeline.**

### Play 5 — Founder/exec LinkedIn organic *(Compounding lever, starts now · owner: execs + marketing support)*
2 execs (or 3 if the appetite exists), 2–3 posts/week each, ghost-supported by marketing from client war stories, podcast clips, calculator data, and delivery POVs. This is the demand-creation layer that makes Q4–Q2 2027 cheaper. It will under-attribute in software — measure via the self-reported source field.
- Also fixes the $22K LinkedIn deal-size problem: content aimed at *embedded team / build-vs-buy decisions*, not "we're hiring/available" posts that attract small placements.

### Play 6 — Mid-funnel conversion repair *(Won-pipeline lever — this is how the $1.08M happens · owner: Marketing + sales leadership)*
$3.4M sits in Develop/Proposal/Contract today at a ~24% win rate by value. Moving win rate and cycle speed is worth more this quarter than any new channel.
- **Proof kit:** 4–6 new written case studies (from the 120+ client base — currently only ~4 public) with quantified outcomes, mapped to the two priority verticals; a references program with 5 clients willing to take calls.
- **Proposal-stage enablement:** the Vendor Evaluation Scorecard doubles as a late-stage tool ("here's how to compare us to anyone"); loss-reason analysis on the 25 lost deals ($2.23M) to find the 2–3 recurring objections; counter-assets for each.
- **Customer-expansion campaign:** Customer Upgrade is the #1 winning source (7 of 10 wins). Marketing runs a deliberate expansion motion — QBR-timed capability content, "what else we do" campaigns into active accounts, new-service announcements. Cheapest won-pipeline dollars available.
- **Ops hygiene:** add `deal_source` values (Lead Magnet, Podcast, Event, Partner Referral vs Customer Referral) + a mandatory "How did you hear about us?" free-text field on every form and discovery call — 30–50% of real sourcing is invisible to software attribution and this is the only reliable way to see it.

### Play 7 — One executive dinner pilot *(Optional stretch · owner: Marketing + leadership)*
One dinner, one city, one vertical, 10–12 CTOs/VPs Eng (podcast guests and target accounts double as the invite list). $8–15K all-in. Events show the highest creation→qualified conversion of any channel and 60–90 day opportunity timelines — a September dinner produces Q4 pipeline and de-risks scaling the format in 2027 planning. Cut this first if capacity is tight.

## 6. What We Are Deliberately NOT Doing in Q3
- **No MQL volume targets, ever.** Hand-raisers from ICP accounts only.
- **No generic gated ebooks/whitepapers** (worst-performing format class).
- **No branded community build** (weakest evidence; participate in existing ones instead).
- **No broad cold email volume plays** (0.5–2% reply; brand damage risk).
- **No ABM platform purchases** (6sense/Demandbase-class tools are overkill at this budget; Clay-class signal tooling + HubSpot is sufficient).
- **No more than 2 verticals** in the Q3 ABM motion — focus is the strategy.

## 7. Measurement & Operating Cadence

**North-star metrics (weekly dashboard in HubSpot):**
1. Pipeline $ created, by `deal_source` (marketing-sourced, referral, relationship, upgrade)
2. Qualified conversations (hand-raisers from ICP-fit accounts) — the new unit of marketing output
3. Won $ and win rate by stage-entry cohort
4. Avg. deal size by source (watch LinkedIn move from $22K toward $75K+)
5. Self-reported source distribution ("how did you hear about us") vs. software attribution
6. Speed-to-response on hand-raisers (target: <1 hour)

**Cadence:** Weekly marketing pipeline review (30 min, vs. targets above) · Bi-weekly marketing/sales deal-support standup (mid-funnel play) · Monthly exec readout (target vs. actual + kill/scale decisions per play).

**Kill/scale rules:** any play with zero qualified conversations by week 6 gets rebuilt or killed; anything producing >$100K pipeline gets budget shifted toward it in Q4 planning.
