# Midwestern — Q3 FY2026 Growth Marketing Strategy (Aug 1 – Oct 31, 2026)

**Prepared for:** Director of Marketing, Midwestern Interactive
**Date:** July 15, 2026
**Goal:** Double marketing-sourced pipeline AND double won pipeline for the team — prioritizing extremely qualified leads over lead volume.

This plan is grounded in (a) Midwestern's actual HubSpot funnel data (pulled July 15, 2026), and (b) five parallel research workstreams: B2B demand gen strategies, high-converting lead magnet examples, ABM/vertical GTM playbooks for services firms, funnel benchmarks and channel economics, and Midwestern's own positioning/competitive landscape. Full research is in `research/`.

---

## 1. Executive summary

**The single most important finding:** the fastest path to doubling pipeline this quarter is *not* a new channel. It's operationalizing acceleration and visibility on top of the three programs already running — the referral program, the podcast, and the verticalized ABM website work — plus launching 2–3 **high-intent lead magnets** designed to qualify, not just capture.

Why: Midwestern's own data shows ~87% of pipeline comes from relationships, customer upgrades, and referrals. Research benchmarks confirm this is the highest-conversion, fastest-closing motion available (referral-sourced deals win at 53% higher rates and close 46% faster — Ebsta/Pavilion, 4.2M opportunities). Meanwhile, demand *creation* plays (new content programs, YouTube, communities, SEO) take 2–4 quarters to produce pipeline — they cannot carry a 90-day doubling mandate.

**The plan in one paragraph:** Systematize the referral engine (milestone-triggered asks, formal incentives, partner ecosystem). Layer a signal-based outbound motion onto the existing ABM account list with a sub-$12k/yr intent stack. Repurpose the podcast guest seat as a target-account meeting engine. Ship three high-intent lead magnets (delivery-data-backed cost calculator, free technical/AI-readiness audit, vendor evaluation scorecard). Run two executive roundtables against the ABM list. Fix attribution so marketing gets credit for what it sources. Accelerate the $3.4M of late-stage pipeline already open, because that — not net-new leads — is where Q3's *won* number actually comes from.

## 4. The seven workstreams

### 4.1 Referral engine systematization — highest priority, lowest cost

The program exists; the gap is systematization. Only ~30% of B2B firms run a *formal* program, and formal programs measurably outperform ad hoc ones. Hinge's research warning: referrals are driven by **visibility of expertise**, not passive waiting.

- **Milestone-triggered asks:** define 3 moments where delivery leads ask for an introduction — project launch, positive QBR, NPS 9–10 response. Target: **15 asks/month**, tracked in HubSpot.
- **Incentive structure:** 10% of first-year *collected* revenue, capped at 12 months (the most common agency structure). For reputation-sensitive referrers (execs, consultants), offer a charitable-donation alternative — it outperforms cash with that audience.
- **Partner ecosystem (the referral program's force multiplier):** build a named list of 40 complementary partners — fractional CTOs (most of their work comes from warm intros; they need delivery partners), design agencies, VC/PE platform teams, and the Gloo ecosystem. Reciprocal lead-routing agreements with the top 10.
- **Compounding loop:** every happy referral client gets asked for (a) a Clutch review, (b) a podcast guest appearance, (c) a case study. One relationship, four assets.

**Q3 target:** $400–500k referral-sourced pipeline; 45 asks made; 10 partner agreements.

### 4.2 Signal-based outbound layered on the ABM list

The verticalized ABM website work is already done — this workstream makes it *actionable* by turning anonymous interest into same-day sales conversations.

- **Stack (~$1,100/mo, <$13k/yr):** Clay Growth ($495/mo) for enrichment + signal orchestration, RB2B ($149–249/mo) for person-level US visitor identification on the vertical pages, Apollo Professional ($79/user/mo) for sequencing + basic intent, LinkedIn Sales Navigator Advanced ($149/user/mo). Skip 6sense/Demandbase — $60k–$200k/yr is disproportionate at our scale.
- **Account list:** 150 accounts in three tiers. Tier 1: 25 named dream accounts (1:1 plays, exec-to-exec). Tier 2: 75 vertical-fit accounts (1:few plays per vertical). Tier 3: 50 signal-triggered (programmatic).
- **Signals that trigger outreach:** target-account visits to vertical/case-study pages (RB2B → Slack alert), hiring surges for engineering/product roles (a buying trigger 60–90 days ahead of vendor research), new funding, new CTO/VP Eng, tech-stack changes, LinkedIn engagement with our execs' posts.
- **SLA:** signal-triggered follow-up same business day. Speed-to-lead research is unambiguous — response within an hour is worth multiples in qualification rate.
- **Quality guardrail:** every sequence anchors to vertical proof (case study, calculator, audit offer) — never generic "we do software" outreach. This is how we fix the $22k-average-LinkedIn-deal problem: target seniority + company size that matches the $75k+ engagement profile.

**Q3 target:** 2–4 opportunities, $150–300k; 100% of Tier 1 accounts with a live signal worked same-day.

### 4.3 Podcast as an ABM instrument

The podcast's highest-ROI use is not audience growth — it's **the guest seat as a meeting engine**. Research consensus: ~10% of strategically-selected guests become referral sources or clients (range 5–15%), and pipeline is generated in the booking/pre-interview relationship, before an episode even airs. Notably, download counts show *zero correlation* with attributed revenue — shows generating $500k+ pipeline averaged only ~2,500 downloads/episode.

- **Guest list = ABM list:** book 8–12 executives from Tier 1/Tier 2 accounts this quarter. Outreach frames their expertise ("not a sales call") — acceptance rates are high because you're offering them content and reach.
- **Follow-up motion:** thank-you within 24h with branded clips and quote graphics; pre-commit guests to sharing; per-guest sales-readiness triage (some go to sales in weeks, others nurture ~6 months); quarterly check-ins.
- **Guesting outbound:** pitch our founder/technical leads onto 5–10 vertical trade podcasts — borrowing established audiences beats building one.
- **Distribution compounding:** every episode → 5–8 LinkedIn native clips (fuels §4.6) + published transcript (fuels AI-search citability, §4.5). Note: the show is currently hard to find via search — publish transcripts and episode pages on the site to fix discoverability.

**Q3 target:** 8–12 target-account guests recorded; 1–2 sourced opportunities; 5+ external guest appearances booked.

### 4.4 Lead magnet portfolio — build 3, not 10 (the prescriptive ask)

Research verdict (full detail + 20 examples in `research/02`): interactive, self-qualifying formats dramatically outperform static gated content, and **the qualification mechanism matters more than the format label**. Given the quality-over-volume mandate, we build bottom-funnel assets whose *completion itself* signals budget, authority, and active intent — and we skip generic ebooks entirely.

**Magnet 1 — Project Cost & Timeline Calculator (flagship).**
Modeled on Netguru's ("based on 200+ apps we've built, median MVP $76k; +HIPAA median $118k"). Ground it in *Midwestern's own delivery data* — that specificity is the credibility moat generalist competitors can't copy. Inputs (scope, platform, integrations, compliance, team model: embedded vs. scoped vs. recruit) are deal-sizing questions — a rep can size the deal from the inputs alone. Instant range shown ungated; detailed breakdown PDF gated. Tire-kickers abandon at question 3; real buyers finish. *We're a dev shop — we build this in-house in 2–3 weeks.*

**Magnet 2 — Free Technical / AI-Readiness Audit (highest qualification).**
A 45-minute senior-engineer review of architecture, tech debt, or AI-readiness. Research: 1:1 audit requesters convert at 50–70% MQL→SQL because requesting one *is* a buying signal — "the audit is a sales meeting dressed as free value." Matches the "talent partner for the AI era" positioning exactly. Cap at 6–8/month to bound senior time; the cap is also positioning ("we take 8 per month").

**Magnet 3 — Vendor Evaluation Scorecard + RFP Template ("Choosing a Development Partner").**
Bottom-funnel capture: only someone actively procuring downloads an RFP template (the Gartner pattern). Cheap to produce (1 week), intercepts active evaluations — including ones where we're not yet on the shortlist. Honest criteria including when *not* to hire a firm like us builds the trust that wins consultative deals.

**Q4 seed (start now, launch next quarter):** an original benchmark report from our own delivery data (the Gong Labs / DORA pattern — e.g., "engineering velocity and AI adoption across 100+ client engagements"). This is the demand-creation asset with PR/citation halo; it will not pay off in-quarter, which is exactly why it's seeded now, not bet on.

**Distribution (a magnet without distribution is a PDF):** vertical page CTAs, exec LinkedIn posts, podcast CTAs, outbound sequences (the "give"), retargeting of vertical-page visitors, partner co-promotion. Multi-step forms throughout (they convert ~3x single-page forms).

**Q3 target:** 5–7 sourced opportunities, $375–525k; audit-request → SQL rate >50%; calculator completion → meeting rate >20%.

### 4.5 Reviews + AI-search presence (cheap, fast, compounding)

Buyers shortlist agencies on Clutch, and increasingly via ChatGPT/Perplexity. Our Clutch profile has **7 reviews** — underweight for our size. Meanwhile, LLMs cite third-party sources ~88% of the time when recommending vendors (earned media + review sites), and directory profiles are ~3x more likely to be cited.

- **Clutch sprint:** 10+ new verified reviews this quarter (source: recently-happy clients + referral clients). Refresh profile services/case studies. Mirror on G2/GoodFirms/DesignRush.
- **AI-search (GEO) hygiene:** ungate case studies; publish podcast transcripts; structured FAQ/comparison content per vertical; pursue 2–3 trade-press placements in target verticals. Track: does ChatGPT recommend Midwestern for "best custom software / embedded team partners for [vertical]"?

**Q3 target:** 17+ total Clutch reviews; 1–2 sourced opportunities; measurable LLM citation for 3+ vertical queries.

### 4.6 Executive events + LinkedIn presence

- **Two executive roundtables/dinners** (mid-Sep, mid-Oct), one per priority vertical. 10–15 executives from Tier 1/2 accounts, 2–3 stakeholders per account (multi-threading by design), no pitch — a customer tells their story. Budget $8–12k each. The differentiator is follow-up: only ~18% of event leads ever get meaningful follow-up industry-wide, so every attendee gets a pre-committed, CRM-tracked sequence within 48 hours.
- **LinkedIn exec cadence:** 2 voices (CEO/founder + a senior technical leader), 3 posts/week each, fed by podcast clips and delivery insights. Personal profiles get ~8x company-page engagement; this is the visibility layer that makes referrals, outbound, and events all warmer. Amplify winners with Thought Leader Ads (~6x more efficient than standard sponsored content) against the 150-account list. Modest budget: $2–4k/mo.

**Q3 target:** 3–5 event-sourced opportunities ($225–375k); 25+ target-account executives engaging with exec content monthly.

### 4.7 Attribution, orchestration & the weekly scorecard

If we don't fix measurement in week 1, we can't prove the doubling in November.

- **HubSpot hygiene:** make `deal_source` required at deal creation (picklist governance); add a required self-reported "How did you hear about us?" field on all forms and discovery calls (dark-social research consistently shows software attribution missing most podcast/community influence); UTM discipline on all campaigns.
- **Definitions everyone signs:** *Marketing-sourced* = first qualified touch was a marketing program (magnet, event, podcast, outbound sequence, directory, inbound). *Marketing-influenced* = any marketing touch pre-opportunity. Report both — sourced for the goal, influenced for the truth. Healthy sales-led orgs run 25–45% marketing-sourced; we're at ~5%, so headroom is enormous.
- **Redefine the MQL as "meeting-worthy":** fit (vertical, size, seniority) + intent action (audit request, calculator completion, event attendance, high-intent page path). **Primary KPI: qualified meetings booked, not MQLs.** Qualify opportunities MEDDPICC-lite (champion, economic buyer, decision process, paper process) given 8–13 person buying committees at our deal size.
- **SLA:** marketing hands off within 1 business day → sales accepts/rejects within 24h with reason codes. Rejects feed back into targeting weekly.
- **Weekly pipeline council (30 min, marketing + sales):** meetings booked, opps created by source ($/#), late-stage movement + slippage, referral asks made, scorecard vs. targets. Same numbers, one dashboard, no parallel spreadsheets.

## 7. Honest caveats (the consultant's fine print)

1. **The won-pipeline doubling is mostly a sales-acceleration outcome, not a lead-gen outcome.** With 120–210-day cycles, marketing's new pipeline this quarter primarily protects Q4/Q1. If late-stage deals slip, the won number misses even if marketing over-delivers. This is why acceleration plays (roundtables, multi-threading, reactivation) are in the marketing plan at all.
2. **Data quality:** the Q2 creation spike is partly pipeline-migration artifact; native HubSpot attribution is broken (88/92 deals "Offline"). Fix in week 1 or November's report is unprovable. Several industry stats in the research are vendor-sourced — the research files flag every number's trust level; don't put the flagged ones in a board deck.
3. **The "marketing-sourced" definition needs executive sign-off up front.** Strict first-touch attribution will undercount podcast/referral/dark-social influence. Recommend reporting sourced + influenced + a win-rate lift comparison (deals with marketing touches vs. without).
4. **Two-vertical focus decision needed:** Midwestern currently runs two identities (Gloo/faith-ecosystem vertical; venture-backed AI/SaaS via You.com, GibsonAI, Lyft proof). The ABM sprint, roundtables, and magnets each need a vertical anchor — pick the two verticals with the strongest case-study proof and buyer density for Q3, and say no to the rest until Q4.
5. **Do NOT start this quarter:** owned community, YouTube channel, net-new SEO program, marketplace/co-sell partnerships (AWS/Azure) as a Q3 dependency. All are legitimate 2–4-quarter plays; all are seeded or deferred deliberately.
