# LinkedIn Humor (the bit post)

Deadpan satire that parodies a recognizable LinkedIn genre from the inside
(advice guru, lessons-learned story, budget flex, engagement bait) while
landing one real point JK actually believes. The reader laughs first, then
realizes the post said something true.

This is written AS JK: a marketing director with a reputation, a CEO, and a
real point to make. Comedy-brand moves (like Hawkins' all-lowercase
absurdism) get adapted, not copied.

## Voice constants

- Sentence case. JK rejected all-lowercase; on him it reads as costume.
- No em dashes ever. Commas, periods, colons, parentheses.
- Contractions yes. Emoji: zero or one, never more.
- Never write his blacklisted constructions: contrast-flips ("it isn't X,
  it's Y" / "didn't X; it Y'd"), validation openers ("You're right to..."),
  compressed chiasmus. Make one side concrete and drop the mirrored clause.
- Anchor bits in his real life: the "pull the right lever at the right time"
  mantra, the 5pm hard stop, Asana, MQL handoffs, his "I'll just do it
  myself" toxic trait. Real furniture is what makes the jokes believable.

## The honesty rule (the big one)

JK will not post fiction about himself as fact. He rejected "I bought a cast
iron lever off eBay" because he didn't buy one.

Allowed:
- Absurd numbers in an obviously satirical frame ("$1.4M a month to automate
  three things"). The absurdity itself is the tell.
- Rituals or habits he actually does, or could honestly start doing ("every
  morning I name the one lever only I can pull").
- Explicitly imaginary things ("Lever pulls: 10/10 (imaginary)").

Not allowed:
- Invented purchases, events, conversations, or personal actions presented
  as real.

If a bit needs a fictional act, make it hypothetical or imaginary inside the
post, or ask JK whether it actually happened. Never quietly commit him to a
lie.

## The toolbox

Pick one primary vehicle per post. Two at most; stacking more reads as
trying too hard.

1. **Literalized metaphor.** Take a business platitude at face value and
   commit. ("Pull the right lever" becomes a daily lever ritual.)
2. **Deadpan stat block.** Track something absurd like a KPI. Fake precision
   sells it: "Lever pulls: 10/10", "down 60%", "$466K per thing". This is
   also where the sincere point hides (see smuggled sincerity).
3. **Confident fake math.** Do the arithmetic and present it proudly.
   "That's $466K per thing." Milk it exactly once.
4. **Performed satisfaction.** State a disaster like a win: "Budget's landed
   in a really good spot." Funnier than stating the number cold.
5. **Self-aware denial.** "Do not annualize it. It stops being a good spot
   when you annualize it."
6. **Escalation mechanism.** One sentence explaining how the reasonable
   thing became absurd: "each tool needs another tool to talk to the rest,
   and one more to tell you if any of them are working." Usually the most
   relatable line in the post; this is the beat that gets shared.
7. **Parody CTA.** Mirror real engagement bait exactly: "Follow for more
   marketing leadership advice." / "Comment SAUCE below and I'll break down
   how we kept the budget this tight." Make the CTA's punchline a callback.
8. **Mundane specificity.** Boring, precise details ($340, cast iron, Order
   #4782). Precision reads as evidence.

## Beat structure

A default skeleton, not a template. Cut beats freely; don't add lanes.

1. Straight setup, totally sincere (1-2 lines). "Been automating more of our
   marketing lately."
2. Mundane list or detail. Short lines, whitespace.
3. Famous-last-words line. "Should be simple. There's a tool for everything
   now."
4. Escalation mechanism.
5. Deadpan reveal (the absurd number or the stat block).
6. Milk it once (fake math or self-aware denial).
7. Callback ending or parody CTA.

## Commitment

Never wink. No "lol", no "just kidding", no explaining the joke, no tidy
LinkedIn lesson at the end. The stat block or the callback IS the lesson. If
you feel the urge to explain the point in prose, the smuggling failed;
rework the stats instead.

## Smuggled sincerity

Every post carries one real observation JK believes (busywork is not
leverage; martech sprawl is out of control). Put it inside the bit's
mechanics, usually as 2-3 true items in the stat block ("Decisions my team
stopped waiting on: all of them", "Drafts I told people to ship: 4")
sandwiched between joke stats. The goal: repostable as "funny AND true."

## QA passes (run all four before handing over)

1. **Logic pass.** Every callback needs an antecedent earlier in the post.
   If the ending is "I pulled the lever," the ritual or object must be
   established first. A punchline with no setup confuses readers before it
   amuses them. (This killed a draft once: hypothetical lever plus "lever
   pulls: 10/10".)
2. **Honesty pass.** Apply the honesty rule. Flag any invented personal act
   to JK instead of shipping it.
3. **Voice pass.** Sentence case throughout, no em dashes, 0-1 emoji, grep
   for blacklist patterns (`isn't [^.;]*, it's`, `didn't [^.;]*; (it|they)`,
   validation openers). JK drafts fast, so also catch typos and dropped
   words.
4. **Cold-reader pass.** Could someone take the absurd claim straight and
   think worse of JK or Midwestern? If yes, say so and suggest a mitigation
   (a clearer tell inside the post, or a heads-up to Matt before posting).
   Satire touching money, layoffs, clients, or named people gets extra
   scrutiny.

If a comment-bait CTA ships ("Comment SAUCE"), hand JK a canned reply too,
since replies are reach: "step one: stop opening the invoices."

## Worked examples

### The lever post (literalized metaphor + honest ritual)

```
There's always more marketing to do.

Which sometimes feels really defeating. But a while ago I quit moping about it and adopted a mantra:

"Pull the right lever at the right time."

Great in theory. Unfortunately, my toxic trait is that I'm an "I'll just do it myself" guy.

I'm working on it. The fix is simple: every morning I name the one lever only I can pull that day. Just one. Then I go pull it.

Two weeks of data:

- Tasks completed by me: down 60%
- Decisions my team stopped waiting on: all of them
- Drafts I told people to ship: 4
- Lever pulls: 10/10

I used to look up at 5pm wondering what I actually did all day.

Now I know. I pulled the lever.

Follow for more marketing leadership advice.
```

Why it works: the mantra is really his; the ritual is real and trackable, so
"10/10" is honest; the delegation stats carry the sincere point; the ending
is a pure callback with no explanation.

### The martech post (deadpan cost flex)

```
Been automating more of our marketing lately.

MQL handoff.
Social tracking.
Analytics.

Should be simple. There's a tool for everything now.

Turns out there are several tools for everything. Each one needs another tool to talk to the rest, and one more to tell you if any of them are working.

But the budget's landed in a really good spot. We're only spending $1.4M a month to automate three things.

That's $466K per thing. Do not annualize it. It stops being a good spot when you annualize it.

Comment SAUCE below and I'll break down how we kept the budget this tight.
```

Why it works: performed satisfaction ("really good spot"), one escalation
mechanism, fake math milked exactly once, self-aware denial, and the CTA's
punchline is a callback ("this tight" vs $1.4M).

## Stacks with

Draft with this skill, then run the humanizer sweep before handoff (JK's
standing rule for final deliverables). casual-tone is for his sincere posts;
this skill replaces it for bit posts. Don't run both on the same draft.

## When drafting fresh (no JK draft yet)

Get from him or from context: the real frustration or observation, plus one
or two true details from his actual week. Then deliver ONE main draft and at
most two one-line alternate angles. JK triages best with few options.
